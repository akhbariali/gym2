## 1. Executive Brand Definition

**WheyClub is an online store for sports nutrition and selected wellness
products, built around reliable sourcing, clear information, and
practical customer guidance.**

The immediate business objective is not to build an elaborate brand or
launch proprietary products. It is to secure reliable supply, make the
first profitable sales, learn what customers actually buy, and then
improve the business using evidence.

**Strategic rule:** Build only as much brand as is needed to earn
customer trust and support a sale. Do not let branding work delay
supplier validation, product availability, or customer conversations.

## 2. Brand Purpose

### Why does WheyClub exist?

The nutrition and supplement market can be confusing. Consumers face
exaggerated marketing claims, incomplete product information,
uncertainty about authenticity, and difficulty comparing products.

WheyClub exists to make healthy and nutrition-related choices easier,
clearer, and more trustworthy.

**Brand Purpose:**

> Making healthy choices easier, clearer, and more trustworthy.

The purpose must guide supplier selection, product information, content,
packaging, customer support, and business decisions.

## 3. Vision

### Long-term ambition

WheyClub may eventually become a trusted Iranian nutrition and wellness
brand with a portfolio of carefully selected products and, if
commercially justified, proprietary products.

This is a direction, not a current operating claim or a reason to invest
heavily today.

### Staged development

1.  **Stage 1 --- Validate:** Find legitimate suppliers, identify a
    small initial assortment, understand unit economics, and make the
    first sales.
2.  **Stage 2 --- Repeat:** Improve fulfillment, customer experience,
    supplier terms, repeat purchases, and acquisition economics.
3.  **Stage 3 --- Differentiate:** Build stronger brand recognition,
    useful comparison tools, content, and customer retention.
4.  **Stage 4 --- Expand selectively:** Consider new categories or
    WheyClub-branded products only when customer demand, compliance,
    quality control, and financial viability are demonstrated.

Do not invest in Stage 3 or Stage 4 as if Stage 1 and Stage 2 have
already succeeded.

## 4. Mission

**Mission statement:**

> We help people make better health decisions through trusted products,
> transparent information, and practical nutrition solutions.

The mission is delivered through four activities:

1.  Selecting and supplying quality products from reliable sources.
2.  Providing accurate, transparent, and verifiable product information.
3.  Publishing practical, evidence-informed education about nutrition,
    supplements, fitness, and healthy living.
4.  Developing proprietary products that address real customer needs.

## 5. Brand Positioning

### Positioning statement

> For people who care about their health, fitness, and nutrition,
> WheyClub is a trusted nutrition and wellness brand that makes choosing
> the right products easier through transparency, product quality, and
> practical knowledge.

### Four positioning pillars

#### 1. Trust

-   Reliable suppliers and documented sourcing.
-   Authenticity information and verification guidance where available.
-   Clear pricing, policies, and delivery information.
-   Honest customer support.

#### 2. Evidence

-   Educational content informed by credible scientific sources.
-   Accurate nutrition and ingredient information.
-   No unsupported health or performance claims.
-   Clear distinction between established evidence, uncertainty, and
    marketing claims.

#### 3. Transparency

-   Serving size and servings per package.
-   Cost per serving and, where relevant, cost per 25 g of protein.
-   Ingredient and nutrition information.
-   Clear stock status, delivery conditions, returns, and product
    limitations.

#### 4. Practicality

-   Product comparison tools.
-   Nutrition calculators.
-   Guides that help customers choose products.
-   Simple explanations that turn information into useful decisions.

**Core competitive principle:** Trust must be demonstrated through real
processes, not merely communicated as a slogan.

## 6. Brand Archetypes

WheyClub should combine two archetypes.

### Primary archetype: The Sage --- 70%

**Role:** The knowledgeable, rational, evidence-oriented guide.

The Sage helps customers understand products and make informed
decisions.

Behaviors: - Explain rather than exaggerate. - Compare products
fairly. - Cite credible sources for scientific claims. - Acknowledge
uncertainty and limitations. - Prioritize accuracy over persuasion.

### Secondary archetype: The Caregiver --- 30%

**Role:** The responsible, supportive, accessible guide.

The Caregiver helps people pursue healthier lives without judgment or
unnecessary pressure.

Behaviors: - Respect different levels of knowledge and fitness. - Offer
practical guidance. - Avoid shaming or unrealistic expectations. -
Recommend against a purchase when the product is unnecessary or
unsuitable.

The Sage provides expertise; the Caregiver makes that expertise
approachable.

## 7. Target Audiences

Segment audiences by needs and buying behavior rather than demographics
alone.

### Segment 1 --- Fitness Beginners

-   New to exercise and supplements.
-   Limited knowledge of nutrition products.
-   Concerned about counterfeit or unsuitable products.
-   Need simple explanations and guidance.

**Opportunity:** Starter kits, beginner guides, product finders, and
educational content.

### Segment 2 --- Active Fitness Consumers

-   Exercise regularly.
-   Already purchase supplements.
-   Compare ingredients, quality, price, and value.
-   Have potential for repeat purchases.

**Opportunity:** Product comparisons, transparent product data,
cost-per-serving tools, and replenishment reminders.

### Segment 3 --- Health-conscious Consumers

-   Interested in nutrition and quality of life.
-   Not necessarily bodybuilders or gym users.
-   May purchase healthy foods, everyday nutrition products, and
    selected supplements.

**Opportunity:** Expand gradually into daily nutrition and wellness
categories.

### Segment 4 --- B2B Partners

-   Coaches, gyms, studios, and relevant businesses.
-   Need reliable supply and clear commercial terms.
-   May purchase repeatedly or in volume.

**Opportunity:** Partner programs, wholesale arrangements, and coach/gym
collaborations.

**Initial priority:** Focus consumer marketing on Segments 1 and 2.
Treat Segment 3 as a growth market and Segment 4 as a business
development channel.

## 8. Brand Architecture and Initial Assortment

Use WheyClub as the single store and master brand. Do not create
multiple sub-brands or a broad category ecosystem at launch.

### Initial assortment

Start with approximately 3--5 products, selected only after supplier and
market checks. Possible categories to investigate:

-   Whey protein
-   Creatine monohydrate
-   One or two other products with clear demand and reliable supply

These are candidate categories, not a commitment to stock them. Choose
products based on verified customer demand, legal eligibility, supplier
reliability, product authenticity, price competitiveness, expiry dates,
and realistic gross margin.

### Private-label strategy

Private label is a later-stage option, not a launch priority.

Do not commission custom manufacturing, packaging, or large
minimum-order quantities until WheyClub has: - Evidence of repeat demand
in a specific product category. - A reliable understanding of customer
requirements and acceptable price. - A qualified manufacturer and
documented quality-control process. - A clear regulatory and licensing
pathway. - A financial case that includes testing, packaging, inventory,
marketing, and working capital.

Until then, resell established, legally marketable products from
traceable sources.

## 9. Tone of Voice

WheyClub should sound like a knowledgeable, responsible specialist---not
an aggressive salesperson.

  Attribute      Desired level
  -------------- ----------------------
  Professional   High
  Scientific     High, but accessible
  Friendly       Moderate
  Promotional    Low
  Emotional      Low
  Confident      High
  Aggressive     None

### Writing principles

1.  Explain scientific concepts in plain language.
2.  Support health and performance claims with credible sources.
3.  Do not hide product limitations or relevant risks.
4.  Avoid exaggerated promises and guaranteed-result language.
5.  Compare products fairly.
6.  Be willing to tell customers when they do not need a product.
7.  Distinguish educational information from medical advice.

### Example of the desired voice

**Good:**

> Whey protein is a high-quality protein source that can help people
> meet their daily protein requirements. However, it is not essential
> for everyone. If you already get enough protein from food, you may not
> need a protein supplement.

**Avoid:**

> Take this whey and experience explosive muscle growth in the shortest
> possible time!

**Modern Scientific Wellness**

Combine: - Scientific credibility. - Natural, balanced wellness. -
Modern, premium design.

Avoid two extremes: - Generic bodybuilding aesthetics with excessive
neon colors, aggressive typography, and muscle imagery. - Generic

### Logo direction

Use a simple, typography-led wordmark.

Requirements: - WHEYCLUB wordmark in uppercase. - A distinctive but
minimal W monogram. - Light and dark versions. - Single-color version
for packaging and labels. - Strong legibility at small sizes. - Avoid
using dumbbells, flexing muscles, or shaker bottles as the main logo
symbol.

### Photography direction

Use: - Realistic product photography. - Controlled studio lighting or
natural light. - Neutral backgrounds and restrained color grading. -
Editorial fitness and nutrition lifestyle imagery. - Clear product-label
photography where product details matter.

Avoid: - Generic stock bodybuilder imagery. - Excessive retouching. -
Artificial-looking AI product labels. - Images that imply product
features or certifications that are not real.

AI-generated imagery may be used for campaign concepts and lifestyle
visuals, but actual product packaging, labels, ingredients, and
certifications must remain accurate.

## 12. Website Redesign Strategy

The website must serve three primary functions:

1.  Sell products.
2.  Build trust.
3.  Help visitors make informed nutrition and product decisions.

The redesign should not be treated as a purely visual exercise. It must
improve information architecture, conversion, usability, and
credibility.

### Proposed website navigation

-   **Home:** Brand introduction and key entry points.
-   **Shop:** Products and categories.
-   **Shop by Goal:** Browse by customer goal.
-   **Smart Tools:** Calculators and product-selection tools.
-   **Learn:** Educational articles and guides.
-   **About Us:** Brand story, sourcing standards, and principles.
-   **B2B & Partners:** Coaches, gyms, and business partnerships.

### Homepage structure

#### Section 1 --- Hero

**Headline:**

> Smarter Nutrition. Better Progress.

**Supporting copy:**

> Everything you need to make better choices for your health, fitness,
> and nutrition.

Suggested CTAs: - Explore Products - Discover Your Needs

The Persian version should be natural and concise, not a literal
translation.

#### Section 2 --- Shop by Goal

Suggested categories: - Muscle Building - Weight Management - Daily
Nutrition - Fitness Essentials

Use clear imagery and short descriptions. Do not imply that a product
category guarantees a specific body-composition outcome.

#### Section 3 --- Featured Products

Each product card should show, where relevant: - Product image. -
Product name and brand. - Price. - Price per serving. - Protein or
active ingredient per serving. - Stock status. - Customer rating, if
based on genuine reviews. - Comparison option.

Preserve the cost-per-serving feature. It is a useful differentiator.

#### Section 4 --- Why WheyClub?

Four core trust messages: - Authentic Products - Evidence-Informed
Information - Transparent Product Details - Real Customer Support

Only make claims that can be substantiated operationally.

#### Section 5 --- Smart Tools

Prioritize: - Protein Calculator. - Calorie Calculator. - Supplement
Finder. - Product Comparison. - Cost-per-Serving Calculator.

A future **WheyClub Guide** can recommend relevant products based on
customer preferences and product data. It must not act as a doctor or
dietitian. It should explain its criteria and limitations.

#### Section 6 --- Learn & Grow

Content categories: - Nutrition Science. - Supplement Education. -
Fitness & Training. - Healthy Lifestyle.

Each article should include: - Author or reviewer. - Credible references
where appropriate. - Publication date. - Last-reviewed date. - Clear
distinction between general education and individualized medical advice.

#### Section 7 --- Trust & Social Proof

Include: - Verified customer reviews. - Real sourcing and authenticity
information. - Clear FAQs. - Transparent returns and delivery
policies. - Genuine contact and support information.

Never invent customer counts, review scores, sales figures,
certifications, or operational capabilities.

## 13. Product Page Standard

Every product page should follow a consistent template.

### Product overview

-   Accurate product name and brand.
-   Product category and package size.
-   Clear product images.
-   Price and stock status.
-   Relevant authenticity information.

### Nutrition overview

Where the information is available: - Serving size. - Protein or active
ingredient per serving. - Total servings. - Cost per serving. - Relevant
nutrition facts.

### Product information

-   Ingredients.
-   Directions for use.
-   Relevant warnings and limitations.
-   Manufacturer information.
-   Authenticity and verification instructions.
-   Storage conditions.

### Why choose this product?

Provide a balanced explanation of: - What the product offers. - Who may
find it useful. - What it does not do. - Relevant alternatives. - Any
important limitations.

Do not invent specifications, nutritional values, certifications, or
authenticity claims.

## 14. Brand Experience and Customer Journey

Design the customer experience as a continuous journey:

1.  **Discovery:** The customer finds WheyClub through search, social
    media, referrals, or a coach.
2.  **Education:** An article, guide, or free tool helps clarify the
    customer's needs.
3.  **Selection:** The customer compares relevant products using
    transparent information.
4.  **Purchase:** Checkout is simple, secure, and clear.
5.  **Post-purchase:** The customer receives order updates, product
    information, and accessible support.
6.  **Retention:** Relevant recommendations, replenishment reminders,
    and loyalty benefits encourage repeat purchases.

### Future customer account

Consider a customer dashboard where users can: - Review past
purchases. - Access product information. - Track orders. - Save
preferred products. - Receive optional replenishment reminders.

Do not make unsupported medical or individualized nutrition
recommendations.

## 16. First-Sales Roadmap

The first 30--60 days should validate whether WheyClub can source and
sell a small number of products profitably. The roadmap is intentionally
commercial and lean.

### Phase 1 --- Supplier discovery and feasibility (Days 1--10)

**Objective:** Confirm that WheyClub can legally and reliably source
products at viable prices.

-   Identify at least 10 potential suppliers, distributors,
    manufacturers, or appropriately licensed partners.
-   Request current price lists, stock status, minimum order quantities,
    invoice arrangements, delivery terms, and product documentation.
-   Ask whether they support small or single-item orders, reseller
    pricing, or direct fulfillment.
-   Verify the supplier's legal identity and the product's applicable
    registration, authenticity, and traceability information.
-   Check the legal requirements for online sale and fulfillment of each
    product category with the relevant Iranian authorities before taking
    orders.
-   Compare supplier prices with current market prices.
-   Calculate contribution margin after product cost, packaging,
    shipping subsidy, payment fees, returns, and customer acquisition
    costs.
-   Reject suppliers who cannot provide credible documentation or
    traceable sourcing.

**Deliverable:** A shortlist of 2--3 credible suppliers and a comparison
sheet.

### Phase 2 --- Small assortment and sales setup (Days 11--20)

**Objective:** Prepare a minimum viable store, not a full-scale brand
experience.

-   Select only 3--5 products with verified supply and sensible
    economics.
-   Confirm how stock availability will be checked before accepting each
    order.
-   Agree on fulfillment, payment, returns, damaged-product handling,
    and customer-support responsibilities.
-   Create accurate product pages using verified specifications only.
-   Show clear prices, package sizes, expiry/stock information where
    appropriate, delivery terms, and contact details.
-   Keep the website checkout and ordering process simple.
-   Use a basic visual identity and the slogan **«مکمل مطمئن، انتخاب
    آگاهانه»**.
-   Do not advertise products that cannot be reliably supplied.

**Deliverable:** A small, operationally honest catalog and a working
order process.

### Phase 3 --- First customer orders (Days 21--30)

**Objective:** Get the first genuine orders and learn why customers buy.

-   Publish practical educational content tied to the initial
    assortment.
-   Talk directly to potential customers and relevant fitness
    communities without spam.
-   Test a small number of product-focused posts and offers.
-   Explain why each product may or may not suit a buyer; avoid
    exaggerated health or performance claims.
-   Track every inquiry, abandoned purchase, order, delivery issue, and
    customer question.
-   Aim for the first 10 genuine orders as an initial learning target,
    not a guaranteed forecast.
-   Do not accept payment unless supply, delivery timing, legal
    eligibility, and refund arrangements are clear.

**Deliverable:** Real customer feedback and order-level financial data.

### Phase 4 --- Repeatability check (Days 31--60)

**Objective:** Determine whether the model can be repeated.

-   Identify which products attract inquiries and which actually sell.
-   Calculate actual contribution margin per completed order.
-   Measure supplier fulfillment reliability and stock accuracy.
-   Ask buyers about product selection, trust, price, delivery, and
    overall experience.
-   Track repeat purchases when enough time has passed for
    replenishment.
-   Improve the assortment and supplier terms based on evidence.
-   Stop promoting products with poor margins, unreliable supply, or
    weak demand.

**Deliverable:** A decision to continue, change the assortment, change
suppliers, or revise the business model.

### Phase 5 --- Decide what to build next (Days 61--90)

Only after the first-sales and repeatability tests:

-   Improve brand identity and website elements that demonstrably affect
    trust or conversion.
-   Consider modest inventory for products with proven demand and
    reliable turnover.
-   Build partnerships with coaches, gyms, or other relevant businesses
    where commercially useful.
-   Add comparison tools or educational resources if customers need
    them.
-   Evaluate additional categories only when they fit customer demand
    and the brand.
-   Treat private label as a research project only; do not commit
    capital without validated demand, compliance, quality assurance, and
    a financial case.

### Supplier outreach checklist

Ask every potential supplier:

1.  Are you the manufacturer, importer, authorized distributor, or a
    reseller?
2.  Can you provide a current price list and stock updates?
3.  What are the minimum order quantity and reseller terms?
4.  Can you supply single units or small quantities?
5.  Can you provide valid invoices and product traceability/authenticity
    documentation?
6.  What are the expiry dates and batch details of available stock?
7.  Who handles shipping, returns, damaged goods, and customer
    complaints?
8.  Is direct-to-customer fulfillment possible, and under what terms?
9.  What are the payment terms and how frequently do prices change?
10. What legal or licensing conditions apply to reselling these products
    online?

### Go / no-go criteria

Do not scale merely because a few orders arrive. Continue only if:

-   Products can be sourced through credible and legally appropriate
    channels.
-   Contribution margin is positive after variable costs.
-   Customers receive what was promised, on time.
-   Stock and price information can be kept accurate.
-   There is evidence of demand beyond friends or family.
-   Customer complaints and returns are manageable.

If these conditions are not met, fix sourcing, pricing, or fulfillment
before spending more on advertising or branding.

## 17. KPIs --- Measuring Early Commercial Progress

Track a small number of metrics from the first inquiry. Do not optimize
vanity metrics such as followers before proving that the store can
fulfill orders profitably.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  KPI                                 What it measures
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Qualified inquiries                 People with genuine purchase intent

  Inquiry-to-order conversion         Share of qualified inquiries that
                                      become orders

  Completed orders                    Orders successfully delivered

  Contribution margin per order       Revenue minus product cost and
                                      variable order costs

  Supplier fulfillment rate           Share of orders supplied as
                                      promised

  On-time delivery rate               Share of orders delivered within
                                      the stated window

  Cancellation and return rate        Operational and product-fit
                                      problems

  Repeat purchase rate                Whether customers return

  Customer acquisition cost           Marketing cost per new customer,
                                      once paid acquisition is tested
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### First milestone

**Target: 10 genuine, completed customer orders with positive
contribution margin.**

This is a learning milestone, not a revenue forecast. The objective is
to discover which products sell, what customers value, whether supply is
dependable, and whether the economics work.

Set larger targets only after establishing a real baseline.

## 18. Non-negotiable Business and Brand Principles

1.  **Sales learning before elaborate branding.** Use a clear, credible
    identity, but do not let design work delay market validation.
2.  **Trust before profit.** Never compromise sourcing, product
    documentation, or customer treatment for a short-term margin.
3.  **Evidence before claims.** Every scientific or product claim must
    have an appropriate basis.
4.  **Transparency before marketing.** Be clear about price, stock,
    delivery, product details, and limitations.
5.  **Supply before promotion.** Do not advertise products that cannot
    be reliably and legally supplied.
6.  **Positive unit economics before scaling.** Understand contribution
    margin, not just gross sales.
7.  **Customer needs before catalog size.** Add products only when there
    is a credible reason to believe customers need them.
8.  **Validate before investing.** Inventory, paid advertising, custom
    packaging, and private-label manufacturing require evidence and a
    financial case.

## 19. Final Brand Summary and Immediate Priorities

### WHEYCLUB

# مکمل مطمئن، انتخاب آگاهانه

**A practical online store for sports nutrition and selected wellness
products.**

WheyClub should initially be: - Small and focused, not a large

1.  Find and verify potential suppliers.
2.  Confirm the legal requirements for the intended sales and
    fulfillment model.
3.  Compare supplier prices, market prices, and order-level economics.
4.  Select 3--5 products with dependable supply.
5.  Set up accurate product pages and a simple ordering process.
6.  Reach the first 10 genuine completed orders.
7.  Review customer feedback, fulfillment quality, and actual margins.
8.  Only then decide whether to hold inventory, expand the catalog,
    invest in marketing, or develop the brand further.

### What not to prioritize yet

-   Custom-manufactured WheyClub products.
-   Expensive packaging or a full visual identity system.
-   A complex website, app, or AI assistant.
-   A large catalog across unrelated categories.
-   Heavy paid advertising before unit economics and fulfillment are
    tested.
-   Large inventory purchases without validated demand.

**Ultimate near-term success criterion:** WheyClub can repeatedly source
a small number of legitimate products, deliver them as promised, satisfy
customers, and retain positive contribution margin.

The long-term brand ambition remains valid, but it must be earned
through actual customer experience and repeatable operations.
